# Zero Food Waste — Grocery App Trend Report

A research-led trend analysis exploring how a mobile app concept could help grocery shoppers make better use of food, plan meals, and discover products approaching their best-before dates.

> **Project status:** Research and concept proposal only. The app was not launched, and the concept has not been validated through user testing. No food-waste reduction or business impact was measured.

## Project overview

This individual university project applied a trend-funnel framework to the Australian supermarket and grocery context. It explored the drivers behind food-waste-prevention services, compared potential customer segments, considered grocery-shopping touchpoints, and translated the findings into opportunities for a possible mobile app.

The research identified people who prepare meals at home as the primary audience to consider, particularly because meal planning, leftover use, food storage, and purchasing decisions can all influence household food waste.

## The challenge

Food waste can create both environmental and financial costs. At household level, waste may be linked to overbuying, spoilage, preparing more food than needed, uncertainty about date labels, and limited meal planning.

The project explored how a digital service could support shoppers with practical information and more deliberate grocery decisions. It did not measure the scale of these problems independently; the report draws on the sources listed in the original assignment.

## Research approach

The report used a six-zone trend-funnel structure:

1. **Trend identity** — explored the idea of a zero-food-waste mobile app in the grocery sector.
2. **Trend drivers** — examined supermarket and technology partnerships, household behaviour, COVID-era shopping changes, sustainability-focused technology, and household size and income.
3. **Target customers** — compared people who eat out, family grocery shoppers, and people who cook at home.
4. **Aspirational touchpoints** — considered generic products, frozen ready-made meals, and organic products in the shopping context.
5. **Trend location** — considered the broader shift towards online grocery shopping and the potential relevance of the trend across markets.
6. **Opportunities** — identified possible directions for a grocery app concept.

The evidence is historical and reflects the sources and context used in the original report. It should not be read as a current market audit.

## Primary audience

**People who prepare meals at home** were selected as the primary audience for the concept. The report considered their need to plan meals, use ingredients efficiently, make value-conscious grocery choices, and understand how to store food.

The original segmentation compared three broad groups:

- People who eat out with friends
- People who shop for groceries for their families
- People who cook meals at home

This is a research-based target-market choice, not a validated user persona. The project did not include primary user interviews or usability testing.

## Opportunities identified

The trend research highlighted five potential opportunities:

- **Smarter purchasing:** explore whether technology could help shoppers make more considered purchasing decisions.
- **Sustainable routines:** support everyday habits that make better use of food.
- **Food-waste awareness:** provide accessible information about avoidable waste.
- **Meal preparation:** help shoppers plan meals and consider ingredients and leftovers already available.
- **Near-expiry promotions:** make discounted products approaching their best-before date easier to discover, if reliable retailer data were available.

These are opportunities proposed by the research, not features that were implemented or evaluated.

## Proposed app direction

The findings informed a possible app concept organised around three practical feature ideas:

### 1. Near-expiry deals
Help shoppers discover discounted grocery products approaching their best-before date. A real service would depend on retailer participation and reliable product and stock information.

### 2. Meal planning
Help home cooks plan meals around ingredients they have and consider how to use leftovers.

### 3. Food-storage guidance
Offer practical guidance to help users store groceries appropriately and keep food fresh. Any detailed food-safety guidance would need to be checked against reliable sources.

These features describe a proposed direction only. Live inventory integration, AI-driven recommendations, and measurable reductions in food waste were not built or tested as part of this project.

## What the project produced

- A six-zone trend-funnel analysis
- A review of trend drivers and relevant examples
- A comparison of three potential customer segments
- An assessment of grocery-shopping touchpoints
- Five opportunity areas for further exploration
- A proposed direction for a food-waste-prevention app

## Limitations and next steps

The work was exploratory and based on secondary research. The app concept was not launched, and its usability, desirability, technical feasibility, and impact were not validated.

Potential next steps would be to:

1. Interview or survey home cooks about their grocery-planning and leftover-management habits.
2. Test low-fidelity prototypes of the proposed features.
3. Explore the feasibility of retailer partnerships and accurate product-availability data.
4. Run a small pilot before making any claims about food waste, shopper behaviour, or commercial impact.
