Introduction:
Marketing insights need to be transformed into an idea. In order to translate marketing insights into
ideas, it is necessary to capture the ‘intellectual information’ from multiple sources and look backward
and forwards. Furthermore, one way to transform insights into ideas is through the trend funnel
framework. Nonetheless, this report aims to assess the student’s ability to apply the trend funnel to
the retail industry in order to identify a new product or service innovation.

Trend forecasting is a process that examines and forms predictions on consumers’ expected buying
habits. Jain (2019) stated that “it helps predict the future direction of something which would affect
the businesses’ decisions and buying sales of an individual.” Furthermore, trend forecasting is vital in
business because it implements the ability to make informed business decisions and develops data-
driven strategies. Flaunter (2020) suggested that when identifying the source, tracing the evolution,
and recognising patterns of trends, allows the forecasters to provide designers and brands with a
‘vision’ of the future.

In the retail industry, the report is leaning towards more in the supermarket industry. This is because
the life cycle stage of supermarkets and grocery stores in Australia is continuously growing. Youl (2021,
p.18) claimed that the industry is doing well is because this industry is growing slightly faster than the
overall economy. Furthermore, Dorfer (2020) affirmed that in Australia, supermarket sales of overseas
products has increased by 60% in March 2020 than in March 2019. Moreover, in trend forecasting, it
is beneficial for grocery stores. For example, according to Supply Chain Management (2014) indicated
that grocery stores will allow the appropriate shelf space for specific products when there is accurately
forecasting customer demands.

     COVID-19 Lockdown
The global crisis of COVID-19 has caused major impact towards the consumers’ behaviour worldwide
and it has changed the people’s routine on how they purchased grocery items. Martin-Neuninger and
Matthew (2020, p.1) claimed that many individuals panic buy products like waster, gloves,
carbohydrates, canned food, hand sanitizer and toilet papers. However, Jribi et al. (2020, p.3939)
pointed out that the pandemic lockdown in Tunisia improved food shopping performances and
pushed toward a positive behavioural change regarding food wastage. Furthermore, the authors
(2020, pp.3939) found out that about 85% of respondents declared that nothing of what they bought
would be thrown out, that is due to how most respondents from the survey had set up a strategy of
saving, storing, and eating leftovers.

     Households
Households are significant contributors to food waste globally. For example, Wenlock et al. (2008,
p.53) stated that “food wastage was significantly influenced by the family composition with adults
wasting more in absolute terms than children; however, larger households waste less food wastage
than smaller households.” However, AVRIS (2021) identified that household food wastage is due to
food spoilage, over-preparing, overbuying, confusion on the food expiration date, and poor meal
planning. Nonetheless, Saven and Swain (2020) found out that household food wastage dropped by
10.4% during lockdown between March and May in the UK.

     Technological-Environmental Companies
In relations to sustainability, sustainable development is closely linked to the use of technology. For
example, Morgan (2020) pointed out that Apple's products are certified zero waste to landfill and have
decreased average product energy use by 70% in the last ten years. However, in food wastage, Cane
and Parra (2020, p.1664) stated that "tech companies are focusing more on the food loss trend
because they want to make better use of their products and services by offering their surpluses and
perishable products at a better price when their products are about to expire."

     Number of households and their income status
As it was mentioned before, households are the primary source of food wastage. However, Baker,
Fear, and Denniss (2009, p.2) pointed out that the data they have implemented revealed that the
extent of food waste is related to more on the number of people living in the house but is also based
on their income status. “Household with four or more occupants waste the least amount of food per
person while people who live by themselves waste the most amount of food.” (Baker, Fear and
Denniss 2009, p.2). However, low-income individuals tend to be better at meal planning and cause
less impact towards food wastage, emphasised by Porpino, Parente, and Wansink (2015, p.626).
Therefore, the trend could be helpful towards individuals who live by themselves and help them with
their grocery shopping and meal planning.

Customers who like to eat at home could be the primary target market and persona for this trend
because home-cooked leftovers are the most significant source of food waste in the retail industry.

Zone 4: Aspirational Touch Points
     Organic Products
The target customers like to buy organic products from their local grocery stores or order from the
app. Customers choose to purchase organic products because they like to be more aware of how their
food is being produced, suggested by Bunzl (2019). However, buying organic food can be expensive in
certain supermarkets. Parnes (N/A) implied that prices tend to be higher for organic than conventional
products. Therefore, the target customer of this trend is highly price-sensitive, as in they don't buy a
lot of organic products unless it’s on sale. Unfortunately, most of the leftovers that the target
customers throw out are organic products. Silvennoinen et al. (2014, p.1066) indicated that one-third
of the food waste in households is mainly from vegetables, fruits, and berries. Nonetheless, Conrad et
al. (2018, p.1) stated that "increasing consumers' knowledge about how to prepare and store fruits
and vegetables properly would be one of the most practical solutions to reducing food waste at
home." Therefore, if the target customers know to store their fruits and vegetables properly, it could
help them reduce their risk of food wastage at home when cooking or buying grocery items.

     Frozen Ready-Made Meals
Even though the target customer of this trend is home-cooked customers, the target customer of this
trend doesn't always have time to cook when they are busy 24/7. In that case, they buy ready-made
frozen meals as their go-to meals and eat them as a dinner or lunch meal. Lal (2020) insinuated that
the benefits of shopping for frozen foods at a grocery store are great because there are few safety
concerns, and items like these are unlikely to get spoiled and unfreeze when driving back home.
Furthermore, Sen, Antara, and Sen (2021, p.2634) implied that the popularity of frozen meals is
because about 94% of the customers say it's easy to cook while 73% of them also say it's safe to eat.
Even though frozen meals can help minimize spoilage at home. Unfortunately, In The Know (2021)
suggested that it could be a disadvantage to the environment. For instance, when home-cooked
customers buy frozen foods as an everyday go-to meal, it could cause a significant impact on the
environment because frozen foods use more packaging than organic products, insinuated by In The
Know (2021).

     Generic products
Pilon (2014) surveyed in Ask Your Target Market's survey that about 17% of respondents had said they
always buy generic products instead of name brand products, while about 23% had been told that
they buy generic products most of the time. Furthermore, Lal (2020) implied what type of generic
items customers like to buy the most, and rice, sugar, and flour are the items bought most of the time.
Therefore, this indicates that customers who purchase these generic things tend to be price conscious
when shopping. Additionally, this suggests in the report that home-cooked customers tend to a lot of
generic products in certain convenience stores because it's cheap and helps them save much money
for future grocery shopping. Fonte (2020) claimed that buying generic products doesn't mean that the
product is lower-quality; it's just that they cost less and help save a lot of money for shopping.

Zone 6: Opportunities
     Adding artificial intelligence for improved purchases
Agbai (2020, p.1) stated that “AI has various actions that could be helpful towards the retail industry,
application actions such as sorting fresh produce, managing supply chain, food safety compliance
monitoring, effective cleaning in place systems, anticipating consumer preference and new product
development with greater efficiency and savings on time and resources.” For instance, if a
supermarket like Woolworths or Coles uses AI algorithms to minimize the amount of energy admitted
to the mixing process, then they can extend the expiry date, which in turn optimises the selling time
in the supermarket and reduces food losses, emphasised by Food Processing (2021).

     Adopt a healthier and sustainable routine
People living a healthy lifestyle would help lower their risk of developing any high-risk illnesses. For
example, Coots (2015) implied that people who eat whole grains, legumes, and fresh produce as their
daily routine could help lower their cholesterol levels, decrease their risks of certain cancers, and
increase their essential nutrients and minerals. Furthermore, it also helps reduce the environmental
issue and keep them from getting adverse problems to their health, pointed out by Silvennoinen et al.
(2014, p.1066). Nonetheless, in the retail industry, grocery stores in Australia can provide eye-catching
signs pushing customers to make more healthier purchases, hand out healthy snacks, and provide free
fruit for hungry customers.

     Create awareness on food waste
Woolworths, Coles, and Aldi can create awareness of the increasing food wastage in their local
community. Because most people in Australia do not know what to do with their leftovers and they
dispose them in the rubbish. Rubbish (2019) stated that “supermarkets can educate consumers on
how to cut food waste in their households.” Septianto, Kemper, and Northey (2020, p.9) indicated
that in their research it demonstrates that food waste reduction awareness depends on how the
supermarket industry messages are being framed towards their audience. Furthermore, the authors

     Meal-prepping
Meal prepping allows people to eat enough portion sizes to satisfy themselves, which is a great way
to reduce food waste. North Shore University Health System (2019) mentions that having a well-
balanced collection of hand-made meals will help individuals regulate what they eat and help them
stay on track with their personal diet goals. Furthermore, people who meal plan make fewer trips to

Therefore, the food waste app trend could implement a 'how to prepare meals for weekly or
fortnightly' which would help individuals on how to make meals based on the supermarket's leftovers
or unused products.

     Promote products that are about to expire
Tonnes of food are thrown out in supermarkets due to poor inventory management like over-ordering
or cosmetic filtering, indicated by Liu and Tan (2020). Furthermore, Food Bank (2021) mentions that
perfectly edible fresh produce is usually thrown away from supermarket shelves because it does not
meet the criteria for customers, as in the customers don't like the product's shape, size, and colour.
However, this type of trend could benefit the supermarket retail industry if the technology trend helps
promote food products that are about to expire. Rubbish (2019) stated that the "supermarket industry
can adopt a practice to sell goods that are about to expire as a promotional offer or as a discount."


Conclusion:
In conclusion, the trend innovation is about zero food waste application. This type of innovation could
target customers who like to eat at home more and cook home-cooked meals in their daily lives. As
mentioned before, New Hope Network Staff (2019) claimed that about 74% of consumers throw
leftovers food prepared at home. Nonetheless, some points make this innovation more appealing to
the supermarket industry. First, this innovative trend focuses on what the target customer buys:
organic products, frozen ready-made meals, and generic products. Home-cooked customers don't buy
a lot of organic products because of how expensive they are. On the other hand, they purchase many
generic items because it helps save a lot of money when grocery shopping and is cheap in a specific
convenience store. Finally, they buy frozen read-made meals as their go-to meal only when they don't
have time to cook at home.

Furthermore, this type of trend would be suitable for customers that go to their nearest grocery
stores, and at the same time, that do online grocery shopping that is available in their area. Next, there
are opportunities that this trend could help improve the supermarket industry in Australia. They are
by adding artificial intelligence for improved purchases, adopting a healthier and sustainable routine,
creating awareness on food waste, meal-prepping, and promoting products that are about to expire.
Finally, this means the supermarket businesses like Coles, Woolworths, Aldi, and Costco could reduce
food waste, send excess food to food recovery organisations, and recycle the remaining food waste.
Therefore, reducing food wastage is not only better for the environment, but it can help Australia's
supermarket businesses save their money.
